# Voice script — 50% in 45 Days

Record the narrator only, in order, in one file. Maintain a calm pace around 145–155 words per minute. Leave a short space between lines for alignment. Read dates and dollar amounts naturally.

## Narrator

1. Fifty percent profit in forty-five days. In Boston, a line is already forming.
2. At the door, people are ready to hand over their savings.
3. Hand over a hundred dollars. Get a hundred and fifty back.
4. The man taking their money has already served time for forgery.
5. His name will become a word for the same trap you can meet on your phone.
6. It started with a scrap of paper.
7. Charles Ponzi came to America in 1903. He landed in Boston.
8. He said he had two dollars and fifty cents left.
9. Odd jobs took him to Montreal. A forged check put him behind bars.
10. Years later, that record would catch up with him.
11. Back in Boston, Ponzi opened a letter from abroad.
12. Inside was an international reply coupon.
13. It paid the postage for a reply from another country.
14. After the war, exchange rates made some foreign coupons cheap.
15. In America, the same coupon bought more postage than it cost abroad.
16. But postage doesn't pay the rent.
17. The cash investors saw came from somewhere else.
18. In 1920, people lined up at his Securities Exchange Company.
19. Early investors did get paid. Later investors supplied the money.
20. Every payout made the danger look smaller. It also made the line longer.
21. The Smithsonian puts the money he took in at about fifteen million dollars.
22. He bought a mansion and a stake in a bank.
23. Then people came back for their money.
24. Ponzi still paid some of them. But someone inside had seen the books.
25. It was his own publicist.
26. The man hired to sell the story was about to break it.
27. William McMasters had been hired to promote Ponzi. He went to the Boston Post.
28. On August second, the Post printed his verdict. Hopelessly insolvent.
29. The coupons couldn't account for the money.
30. The paper found his Canadian prison record, too.
31. Ponzi was arrested. The bank he had invested in failed.
32. The payments stopped. The people in that line were left with the losses.
33. He pleaded guilty to federal mail fraud. The Boston Post won a Pulitzer.
34. The coupon was real. The profits people were promised were the trap.
35. Here's what that trap looks like today.
36. A trusted friend forwards a pitch. A slick app shows a balance climbing.
37. Say the pitch turns one hundred dollars into a hundred and fifty in forty-five days.
38. Ask what generated that gain, and whether you can actually withdraw it.
39. The SEC flags guaranteed returns, gains that never dip, secret strategies, unregistered sellers, and trouble withdrawing your money.
40. Before sending a dollar, check the person and the firm at investor dot gov.
41. A century later, the line is still there. It moved onto your phone.
42. Fifty percent in forty-five days. Would you have stood in that line?

## Character voices (Kokoro)

These brief exchanges are dramatized. The narrator records none of them.

- **GUARD** (agent) — Name?
- **PONZI** (scammer) — Charles Ponzi.
- **INVESTOR** (worker) — How do you turn stamps into cash?
- **PONZI** (scammer) — That's the secret.
- **MCMASTERS** (banker) — He's at least two million dollars in debt.
- **EDITOR** (ceo) — Put it on the front page.
