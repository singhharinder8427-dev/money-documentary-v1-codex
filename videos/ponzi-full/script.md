# 50% in 45 Days - full script (~4.5-5 min)

**Alternative titles:** The Man Who Named the Scam · The Coupon King · The Line Around the Block

**Angle:** follow the con man (owner liked the test), then turn to the viewer's wallet. **Motif:** the line of people + "fifty percent in forty-five days" (opens and closes the video).

**Length:** 606 words ≈ 3.9 min of speech; with title card, 5 chapter cards, pauses and the end-screen section ≈ 4.5-5 min.

**Planted -> paid off:** the Canadian prison cell (Ch1 -> Ch4) · $2.50 on arrival -> $75 at death · "my secret" -> the coupon math · the line (opening -> ending).


## HOOK
- **NARRATOR:** Boston, July 1920. A line of people stretches down the block.
- **NARRATOR:** Clerks. Factory workers. Housemaids. All holding cash.
- **NARRATOR:** They've been promised fifty percent profit in forty-five days. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** The man taking their money has a prison record. Nobody in that line knows it.
- **NARRATOR:** Soon his name will become the word for this scam. And the same trick is still hunting your money today.
- **NARRATOR:** So how did one small man in a straw hat fool an entire city?

## CHAPTER 1 - TWO DOLLARS AND FIFTY CENTS
- **NARRATOR:** Charles Ponzi stepped off a ship in Boston in 1903.
- **NARRATOR:** By his own account, he had two dollars and fifty cents left. [COUNTER 0→2.5 gold]
- **NARRATOR:** He drifted from job to job. Then north, to Canada. And into a prison cell, for forgery.
- **GUARD:** Name? (dramatised)
- **PONZI:** Remember it. One day you'll read about me. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** Remember that cell. Later, a newspaper will find it.
- **NARRATOR:** By 1919, he was back in Boston. Charming. Broke. And about to open a very lucky letter.

## CHAPTER 2 - THE LOOPHOLE
- **NARRATOR:** Inside was a little slip of paper. An international reply coupon.
- **NARRATOR:** Created in 1906, it let you prepay someone's reply postage, anywhere in the world.
- **NARRATOR:** But after the war, money in Europe lost value. The coupon's price didn't change.
- **NARRATOR:** So buy coupons cheap in Italy. Trade them in America for more stamps.
- **NARRATOR:** On paper, it was legal. Here's the catch: you can't pay rent with stamps.
- **INVESTOR:** So how do stamps turn into cash? (dramatised)
- **PONZI:** That, my friend, is my secret. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** He never explained it. People invested anyway.

## CHAPTER 3 - FORTY-FIVE DAYS
- **NARRATOR:** January 1920. The Securities Exchange Company opens its doors.
- **NARRATOR:** The first investors really did get paid. With money from the next investors.
- **NARRATOR:** Then those, with the money from the next. It only works while the line keeps growing.
- **NARRATOR:** By the end of July, nearly ten million dollars had poured in. [COUNTER 0→10,000,000 gold]
- **NARRATOR:** He bought a mansion. And a big stake in a Boston bank.
- **NARRATOR:** Then the crowd got nervous. And came back for their money.

## MIDPOINT TWIST
- **NARRATOR:** Ponzi paid them, smiling. But what nobody knew was who would sink him.
- **NARRATOR:** Not the police. His own publicist. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** A man he had hired just ten days earlier.

## CHAPTER 4 - THE INSIDER
- **NARRATOR:** William McMasters was hired to make Ponzi look good. He looked at the books instead.
- **MCMASTERS:** He's not rich. He's two million dollars in the hole. (dramatised)
- **EDITOR:** Then put it on the front page. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** On August 2nd, the Boston Post called Ponzi hopelessly insolvent.
- **NARRATOR:** The math was brutal. His plan needed 160 million coupons. About 27,000 existed. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** Then the paper found that old prison cell in Canada.
- **NARRATOR:** Within days, Ponzi was arrested. His bank failed. Other banks went down with it.
- **NARRATOR:** Investors lost an estimated twenty million dollars. Many got back less than thirty cents on the dollar. [COUNTER 0→20,000,000 orange]
- **NARRATOR:** Ponzi pleaded guilty to mail fraud. The newspaper won a Pulitzer. He won five years.
- **NARRATOR:** He was deported to Italy, and died in 1949 in a charity ward in Rio. He left seventy-five dollars.

## CHAPTER 5 - YOUR WALLET
- **NARRATOR:** That was his ending. Here's where this reaches your money.
- **NARRATOR:** In 2009, Bernie Madoff pleaded guilty to the biggest Ponzi scheme in history. His statements showed investors about sixty-five billion dollars. Most of it never existed.
- **NARRATOR:** And in 2024 alone, Americans told the FBI they lost over six and a half billion dollars to investment fraud. [COUNTER 0→6,570,000,000 orange]
- **NARRATOR:** The costume changes. Crypto apps. Trading groups. A friend's can't-lose fund. The trick doesn't.
- **NARRATOR:** The SEC's red flags. Guaranteed high returns with little risk. Returns that never go down. A secret strategy nobody can explain. A seller who isn't registered. Trouble getting your money out.
- **NARRATOR:** Before you invest a dollar, look them up at investor dot gov.

## CALLBACK ENDING
- **NARRATOR:** A century later, the line is still there. It just moved onto your phone.
- **NARRATOR:** Fifty percent in forty-five days. Would you have stood in that line? [CARD]
