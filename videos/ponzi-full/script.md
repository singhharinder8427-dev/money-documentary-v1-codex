# 50% in 45 Days

**Alternative titles** — The Coupon King · The Promise in the Window · The Line Around the Block

**Length** — 484 spoken words. Target under five minutes including the outro and end screen. The Windows preflight checks the actual durations before the preview.

**Angle** — Follow the promise from the 1920 Boston queue to the same pitch on a phone. The coupon, the prison record, and the queue each return later in the story.

## Hook

- **NARRATOR:** Fifty percent profit in forty-five days. In Boston, a line is already forming.
- **NARRATOR:** At the door, people are ready to hand over their savings.
- **NARRATOR:** Hand over a hundred dollars. Get a hundred and fifty back.
- **NARRATOR:** The man taking their money has already served time for forgery.
- **NARRATOR:** His name will become a word for the same trap you can meet on your phone.
- **NARRATOR:** It started with a scrap of paper.

**Title card** — 50% in 45 Days


## 1. Two Dollars and Fifty Cents

- **NARRATOR:** Charles Ponzi came to America in 1903. He landed in Boston.
- **NARRATOR:** He said he had two dollars and fifty cents left. [gold counter to $2.50]
- **NARRATOR:** Odd jobs took him to Montreal. A forged check put him behind bars.
- **GUARD:** Name? [dramatized]
- **PONZI:** Charles Ponzi. [dramatized]
- **NARRATOR:** Years later, that record would catch up with him.
- **NARRATOR:** Back in Boston, Ponzi opened a letter from abroad.

## 2. The Loophole

- **NARRATOR:** Inside was an international reply coupon.
- **NARRATOR:** It paid the postage for a reply from another country.
- **NARRATOR:** After the war, exchange rates made some foreign coupons cheap.
- **NARRATOR:** In America, the same coupon bought more postage than it cost abroad.
- **NARRATOR:** But postage doesn't pay the rent.
- **INVESTOR:** How do you turn stamps into cash? [dramatized]
- **PONZI:** That's the secret. [dramatized]
- **NARRATOR:** The cash investors saw came from somewhere else.

## 3. Forty-Five Days

- **NARRATOR:** In 1920, people lined up at his Securities Exchange Company.
- **NARRATOR:** Early investors did get paid. Later investors supplied the money.
- **NARRATOR:** Every payout made the danger look smaller. It also made the line longer.
- **NARRATOR:** The Smithsonian puts the money he took in at about fifteen million dollars. [gold counter to $15,000,000]
- **NARRATOR:** He bought a mansion and a stake in a bank.
- **NARRATOR:** Then people came back for their money.

## Midpoint twist

- **NARRATOR:** Ponzi still paid some of them. But someone inside had seen the books.
- **NARRATOR:** It was his own publicist. [text card]
- **NARRATOR:** The man hired to sell the story was about to break it.

## 4. The Insider

- **NARRATOR:** William McMasters had been hired to promote Ponzi. He went to the Boston Post.
- **MCMASTERS:** He's at least two million dollars in debt. [dramatized]
- **EDITOR:** Put it on the front page. [dramatized]
- **NARRATOR:** On August second, the Post printed his verdict. Hopelessly insolvent.
- **NARRATOR:** The coupons couldn't account for the money. [text card]
- **NARRATOR:** The paper found his Canadian prison record, too.
- **NARRATOR:** Ponzi was arrested. The bank he had invested in failed.
- **NARRATOR:** The payments stopped. The people in that line were left with the losses.
- **NARRATOR:** He pleaded guilty to federal mail fraud. The Boston Post won a Pulitzer.
- **NARRATOR:** The coupon was real. The profits people were promised were the trap.

## 5. Your Wallet

- **NARRATOR:** Here's what that trap looks like today.
- **NARRATOR:** A trusted friend forwards a pitch. A slick app shows a balance climbing.
- **NARRATOR:** Say the pitch turns one hundred dollars into a hundred and fifty in forty-five days.
- **NARRATOR:** Ask what generated that gain, and whether you can actually withdraw it.
- **NARRATOR:** The SEC flags guaranteed returns, gains that never dip, secret strategies, unregistered sellers, and trouble withdrawing your money.
- **NARRATOR:** Before sending a dollar, check the person and the firm at investor dot gov.

## Callback ending

- **NARRATOR:** A century later, the line is still there. It moved onto your phone.
- **NARRATOR:** Fifty percent in forty-five days. Would you have stood in that line? [text card]
